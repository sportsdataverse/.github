# Venue rules

Record the venue's **official** rules in the repo (`VENUE.md`), with the URL
and the date they were checked, before writing to length. A house cap is not the venue's rule.
A missing `VENUE.md` is a *concern* when the rules are met anyway. **Breaking
a hard rule is a FAIL that ranks above every other finding:** over length,
too many figures, a broken anonymity rule, or a private repository where the
venue requires an open one. It blocks submission, while any other defect only
weakens it.

## What to record

- Deadline with timezone; each phase (abstract, full paper, poster, talk)
- Length unit and what counts toward it (words incl. title? pages incl. refs?)
- Figure/table limits per phase
- Anonymity (blind review? author block allowed?)
- Open-source / repository requirement, and whether the repo must be **public** at submission
- Template/format (Quarto, LaTeX, PDF only), page size, fonts
- Selection criteria as the venue states them

## MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference (checked 2026-10-01, SSAC27)

Source: <https://www.sloansportsconference.com/research-paper-competition>

- Abstract due 2026-10-01 23:59 ET; < 500 words **including title**; at most two
  tables/figures combined.
- Selected on novelty, academic rigor and impact. Full-paper invitations come in
  late October; full papers are due 2026-12-04 23:59; finalists in late January;
  posters in early February.
- **Open source required:** a link to a GitHub (or other) repository supporting
  the research. A private repo doesn't meet this.
- Anonymity: not stated on the public page. Check the submission portal.

ML-venue advice from generic paper skills (NeurIPS checklists, blind-review
hygiene, 8-page LaTeX budgets, "lead with the method") does not transfer to SSAC
automatically. SSAC readers are practitioners, so lead with the decision or the
number a team would use.
